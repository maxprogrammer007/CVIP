Robust Detection of AI-Generated Art via Explainable
AI-Driven Defense Against Adversarial Attacks
Abhinav Shukla
CSVTU Bhilai && ISI Kolkata && IIITH
February 16, 2026
Abstract
The rapid advancement of text-to-image diffusion models has enabled the creation of highly
realistic and artistic images, raising critical challenges for digital forensics, content mod-
eration, and intellectual property protection.
However, state-of-the-art detectors of AI-
generated content, including commercial systems like Hive Moderation, remain highly vul-
nerable to adversarial perturbations—subtle, imperceptible noise that fools classifiers while
preserving visual quality. Existing defenses predominantly treat detection models as black
boxes and fail to address the root cause: over-reliance on fragile, high-frequency artifacts
that are easily disrupted.
This work proposes a novel defense paradigm that leverages Explainable AI (XAI) techniques
to guide detectors toward semantically meaningful features. By generating explanation maps
that reveal regions of vulnerability, we aim to train robust detectors that prioritize stable,
human-interpretable artifacts characteristic of AI-generated art.
1
Background and Motivation
Recent studies, such as the “Organic or Diffused” attack [1], have demonstrated that even leading
detectors (e.g., Hive Moderation, CLIP-based classifiers, and frequency-domain methods) exhibit
near-complete failure under carefully crafted adversarial perturbations on images generated by
modern diffusion models (Stable Diffusion 3, Midjourney v6, Flux, etc.). These attacks exploit
the detector’s dependence on high-frequency noise patterns introduced during the generative
process—patterns that can be trivially removed or altered without affecting perceptual quality.
Current defense strategies—adversarial training, input preprocessing, and ensemble methods—offer
limited robustness and often come at the cost of clean accuracy. Crucially, they do not enforce
learning of the “right” features for the “right” reasons, leaving detectors susceptible to adaptive
attacks.
2
Problem Statement
Given an AI-generated artwork x ∈X produced by a text-to-image diffusion model and a
binary detector fθ : X →{0, 1} (where 1 denotes AI-generated), adversarial attacks construct
a perturbed sample x′ = x + δ (∥δ∥p ≤ϵ) such that fθ(x′) ̸= fθ(x) while x′ remains visually
indistinguishable from x.
We formally state the research problem as follows:
How can Explainable AI techniques (e.g., Grad-CAM, SHAP, or Integrated Gra-
dients) be leveraged to generate explanation maps that identify pixel regions most
vulnerable to adversarial perturbations in AI-generated art, and how can these maps
1
be incorporated as supervisory signals to train detection models that are robust by
design?
3
Research Objectives
The primary objective is to develop a defense mechanism that explicitly shifts the detector’s re-
liance from fragile high-frequency artifacts to semantically meaningful, attack-resistant features,
including but not limited to:
• Consistency in brushstroke patterns and texture flow,
• Anatomical coherence (e.g., realistic hand and finger structures),
• Pupil shape, eye reflections, and lighting realism,
• Overall stylistic and compositional harmony.
Specific goals include:
1. Generate and analyze explanation maps on clean and adversarially perturbed images to
quantify vulnerability.
2. Design training objectives (e.g., explanation consistency regularization, attention masking,
or contrastive alignment) that penalize reliance on unstable regions.
3. Evaluate the resulting detector on clean accuracy, robustness under bounded ℓ∞/ℓ2 at-
tacks, and explanation fidelity (shift toward semantic regions).
4
Expected Contributions
• A novel XAI-guided training framework for robust detection of AI-generated art.
• Empirical evidence that semantic features yield superior adversarial robustness compared
to black-box defenses.
• Insights into interpretable failure modes of current detectors, advancing the intersection
of explainable AI and adversarial robustness.
